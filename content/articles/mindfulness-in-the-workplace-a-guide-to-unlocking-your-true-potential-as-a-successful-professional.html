Every moment you are making three decisions, as Tony Robbins would say:

  1. What to focus on,
  2. What it means, and
  3. What you are going to do about it.

If you are not mindful or conscious, then your life will continue to run on autopilot and you will tend to languish.

How do you feel when you are talking to someone, and that individual’s mind is somewhere else? We have all experienced some interaction at the workplace where we were trying to explain or present something with full passion, but the receiver is not actually focusing on what you are saying. How many times have you been guilty of doing the same?

In this era of multitasking and hyper pressure, many professionals struggle to focus on the present (they keep thinking about the next meeting or next assignment). It’s easy to feel overwhelmed and anxious. However, with mindfulness, you can take control of your thoughts and emotions to navigate the workplace and unlock your true potential as a successful professional.

Over the past 20 years, I have had the privilege of working with many leaders (including management board members of big corporations). The leaders that I admire always made me feel listened to; they were always fully present. This in turn made me feel appreciated and energized.

I am sure you all have come across the term “mindfulness”. I have seen this term triggering different responses from different colleagues at work. While many have mentioned to me that being mindful is extremely helpful, some have mentioned the concept is too difficult to understand and practice in real life. Yet it is essential for professionals who want to improve focus, creativity, and business relationships, and some business leaders, such as Mark Bertolini of Aetna, credit it for the clarity of their business decisions.

This article covers what mindfulness is, its benefits in the workplace, practical tips for your daily routine, and real-world examples from business. And so, let me define mindfulness for you …

Defining Mindfulness and some useful resources

I have heard people saying that “you need to be a meditation guru to be good at being mindful”. Of course, if you practice meditation regularly (focused meditation) it would help, but you can start being mindful right now just by focusing on the present moment. The question is how often can you catch yourself when you go into autopilot mode?

While there are a number of books on mindfulness, I found a few books which made the concept very easy to understand:

  1. Search Inside Yourself, by Chade-Meng Tan. Meng initially developed an internal program for Google, and after a huge success within Google he released this book.
  2. Rewire Your Mind, by Dr. Shauna Shapiro
  3. The Joy of Living, by Yongey Mingyur Rinpoche. The topic of this book is a bit wider but I am sure you will have fun reading it.

I was also fortunate enough to participate in the online course “Search Inside Yourself”.

Don’t fall into the three traps

The interesting point is that just being present may not serve the full purpose. This is where the book Mindfulness by Ellen Langer (she studied at NYU and Yale and then served as a tenured professor at Harvard) provides excellent insights which are genuinely applicable for professionals.

The key essence of her message is that one needs to pay attention in a focused way: use your brain to make sense of what is happening, not just go with the flow. According to Ellen Langer there are three traps one may fall into which would actually result in being mindless:

  1. Trapped by Categories: We tend to put everything into a specific box so that our life becomes easy by following what is already tried and tested. Just because something falls into a specific category we are familiar with does not mean it is correct under specific circumstances. In this way we fail to create new categories to move things forward.
  2. Automatic Behavior: Even if we think that we are being mindful, on many occasions we fall into automatic behavior and in turn fail to challenge the status quo. It is ok to follow a tried and tested path but there are occasions one needs to form new behaviors.
  3. Acting from a Single Perspective: Often we see a phenomenon and interpret it from a single perspective. In those cases even if you think you are being mindful, you are truly not thinking things through.

If there is sufficient interest, then in future I would elaborate on the above three points further.

What is Workplace Mindfulness?

Mindfulness in the workplace means being fully engaged in your work and surroundings, with an open, curious, and compassionate attitude. It allows you to become more aware of your thoughts and feelings without judging or labeling them.

We all possess some level of mindfulness, whether we know it or not. Mindfulness is an innate quality that you can develop with regular practice. Over time, mindfulness can help you move toward a flourishing, productive state. Studies of eight-week mindfulness programs have found changes in the amygdala (a brain region that reacts to stress) alongside lower reported stress. The brain’s ability to form new connections is called neuroplasticity, and mindfulness practice is one way of putting it to work.

Professionals who practice mindfulness can effectively manage stress, make informed decisions, and engage in innovative thinking, which are crucial qualities of a thriving organization.

How Mindfulness Can Help a Business

With the rising number of sick days related to stress, depression, and anxiety, mindfulness may help.

Workplace mindfulness can reduce staff turnover and absenteeism and improve employee productivity and job satisfaction, which can mean attractive cost savings for the company.

7 Benefits of Mindfulness in the Workplace

There are numerous potential benefits to incorporating mindfulness practices into your daily work routine.

  1. Boost your creativity and innovation by enhancing your cognitive flexibility and idea generation.
  2. Improve your ability to make decisions and solve problems by sharpening your focus, ignoring distractions, and thinking creatively.
  3. Reduce workplace absences and increase productivity by managing stress and overwhelm.
  4. Support stronger financial performance and return on investment for you and your organization.
  5. Build stronger and more collaborative relationships with colleagues by increasing social belonging.
  6. Develop resilient leadership capabilities by cultivating emotional intelligence and empathy.
  7. Experience less stress, anxiety, and overwhelm in the workplace.

A Few Practical Tips for Corporate Mindfulness

Tip 1: Build mindfulness into your daily routine

It can be simple and effective. The longer you stick with mindfulness practice, the more profound the experience will be. Over time, gradually increase the amount of time you dedicate to your mindfulness practice, just like when training a muscle.

To me, this is about getting off of autopilot and taking charge of your decisions and actions. This helps you to flourish in life.

Tip 2: Set aside a few minutes each day

Designate a few minutes each day for focused meditation or breathing exercises, even if it’s just five minutes. Set an intention to focus on the present moment and engage fully in your work. To bolster this intention, concentrate on one task at a time. Additionally, recharge your mental energy by taking regular breaks. Before starting any meeting take 3 breaths and ask yourself why you are in the meeting and what exactly you want to get out of it (this will keep you mindful during the meeting).

Tip 3: Practice mindfulness with colleagues at work

Pay attention to how you communicate with your colleagues in the workplace. Actively listening to what they have to say and responding thoughtfully will bring you into the present moment and stop the running narrative in your head. Awareness of the present moment will welcome new ideas and perspectives at work while maintaining an open and curious spirit.

Real-World Examples of Mindfulness in Business

Major organizations such as Target and General Mills are embracing the idea of workplace mindfulness in their corporate systems.

Google has been promoting mindfulness since 2007. Their Search Inside Yourself course is a range of mindfulness-based programs offered to employees.

The program includes mindfulness meditation, self-awareness exercises, and compassion practices. Google describes the course as very successful, and demand among employees has been high.

Aetna, a top health insurance company, runs a “Mindfulness at Work” program that trains employees in mindfulness through meditation and yoga classes. In Aetna’s own study of 239 volunteer employees, participants reported 28% lower stress and gained an average of 62 minutes of productivity a week. Aetna also reported lower healthcare costs, though it says they cannot be fully attributed to the program.

Overall, mindfulness can help you reach your full potential and advance your career. Build it into your daily routine and it can improve your focus, decision-making, creativity, and overall well-being.
